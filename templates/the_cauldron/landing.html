    The Method

    How the difficulty rises

    The Forge runs an autoregulated double-progression engine (APRE-style): the last working set of every exercise is an all-out test, and its result decides your next session. Two levers turn — climb the ladder, or add load — and the engine picks the right one for the movement and the gear you own.

    Lever 1 — Difficulty

    Climb the movement ladder

    For pure bodyweight work, harder is the progression. Hit the top of your rep range for two sessions running and the next rung is earned.

    But it never jumps for you: the unlock is parked as a celebration you Claim or hold. Fall below the range and the Forge regresses you a rung to rebuild — no grinding reps you can't do with form.

    Lever 2 — Load

    Add the smallest real increment

    When you own loadable gear — dumbbells, a barbell, bands — the Forge switches to load progression: beat the top of the range and it adds the smallest weight you actually have, then resets your reps.

    This solves the hardest problem in bodyweight training — overloading the legs and hips — and de-loads automatically when a set comes up short.

    Measured, not guessed

    Objective from the first rep

    Both levers are driven by a real AMRAP count, not a subjective “how hard was that?” Beginners misjudge proximity to failure by 4–5 reps, so the Forge measures the number and adapts from fact.

    Blocked a move for injury or preference? It's swapped for the closest equal-or-easier substitute in the same pattern — the ladder bends around you.

    Recovery, dialled in

    Rest matched to the work

    Rest isn't an afterthought. Heavy, low-rep and loaded compounds get ~2½ minutes; hypertrophy ranges ~2; endurance sets ~75 seconds; isometric holds ~60 — each grounded in the training literature.

    The result: progress you can sustain, on a schedule your body can actually recover from.
